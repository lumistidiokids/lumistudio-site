Focus Games for Kids for Kids Ages 2-5

Little kids build attention the same way they build muscles: a few minutes at a time, through play. These 10 games take no supplies and about 5 minutes. Pick one a day, stop while it is still fun, and print the list for the fridge.

  1. What Is Missing?. Put 3 or 4 toys on the table. Your child closes their eyes while you hide one. Which toy is gone? Add more toys as it gets easy.
  2. Copy My Clap. Clap a short rhythm: clap, clap, pause, clap. Your child copies it. Then switch roles and let them lead.
  3. Slow Simon Says. Play Simon Says very slowly, one move at a time: Simon says touch your nose. Leave out "Simon says" now and then and see who notices.
  4. Red Light, Green Light. Green light means walk, red light means freeze. Stopping on the signal is great practice for self-control.
  5. I Spy a Color. Say: I spy something red. Your child looks around the room to find it. For older kids, use shapes or first sounds.
  6. Freeze Dance. Play music and dance. When the music stops, everybody freezes like a statue until it starts again.
  7. Listening Minute. Sit very still and quiet together. How many sounds can you hear? A bird, a car, the fridge? Name them afterwards.
  8. Two-Step Directions. Give two steps in a row: touch your toes, then jump. When that is easy, try three steps.
  9. Sock Match. Mix up a few pairs of socks and ask your child to find each pair. Sorting asks kids to look closely at details.
  10. Back Drawing. Draw a circle, line or letter on your child's back with your finger. Can they guess what it is? Then they draw on yours.

Tips that make it work

  • Keep games short: 3 to 5 minutes is plenty for a toddler.
  • Move first, sit second: a quick wiggle game helps kids settle for a calm one.
  • Stop while your child is still having fun, so they want to play again tomorrow.
