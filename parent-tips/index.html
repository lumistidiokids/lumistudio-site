Quick tips for parents

Small ideas you can use today. Read the full articles in our Parent Corner.

Learning

  • Stop while it is still fun. A child who wants more tomorrow is the goal.
  • 10 to 15 minutes of table time is plenty for a 3 year old.
  • Teach letter sounds along with letter names: 'B says buh'.
  • Start with the letters in your child's name.
  • Repetition is not boring for toddlers. Read the same book again.
  • Count everything: stairs, grapes, socks, cars.

Focus

  • Two minutes of jumping before a quiet task helps wiggly kids settle.
  • Clear the table. Fewer things means fewer distractions.
  • One task per page works better than busy pages.
  • Sit next to your child. Your presence helps them focus.
  • Praise effort: 'You kept trying!'

Feelings

  • Name the feeling out loud: 'You are sad because...'
  • Practice calm breathing when everyone is calm, not only during a meltdown.
  • A calm corner with a pillow and a soft toy gives a safe place to settle.
  • Talk about what happened later, when everyone is calm.

Hands

  • Short, broken crayons encourage a better grip.
  • Play dough, stickers and threading build strong fingers for writing.
  • Trace with a finger first, then with a crayon.
  • No scissors needed: tearing paper is great practice for little hands.

Routine

  • Keep the order of the day the same, even if times change.
  • Give a warning before transitions: 'Two more minutes'.
  • Put learning time in the morning, when kids are fresh.
  • A picture schedule on the fridge helps kids know what comes next.

Screens

  • Keep a box of printables and crayons for 'I am bored' moments.
  • Watch together and talk about what you see when screens are on.
