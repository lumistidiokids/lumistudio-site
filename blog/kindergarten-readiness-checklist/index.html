Kindergarten Readiness Checklist: 24 Skills to Practice at Home (Free Printable)

Is my child ready for kindergarten? If you are asking this question, you are not alone. Many parents start to wonder about it a year before school begins. The good news: kindergarten readiness is not about reading early or writing perfect letters. It is about a group of everyday skills that help a child feel calm and confident in a classroom.

Below is a simple kindergarten readiness checklist with 24 skills in six areas. You can also download it free as a printable PDF (US Letter and A4) and tick each skill with your child.

What does "kindergarten ready" really mean?

Teachers often say that the most helpful skills are not academic. A child who can take turns, ask for help, open a lunchbox and listen to a short story will settle into kindergarten much more easily. Letters and numbers matter too, but they grow fast once a child feels safe and curious.

Every child grows at their own pace. A "not yet" on this list is normal, especially for younger 4 year olds. Use it as a friendly guide, not a test.

The 24-skill kindergarten readiness checklist

1. Self-care

  • Uses the bathroom on their own and washes hands
  • Puts on a coat and shoes, tries zippers
  • Opens a lunchbox and snack containers
  • Asks a grown-up for help when needed

2. Feelings and friends

  • Takes turns and shares with other children
  • Follows simple daily routines
  • Stays with a teacher or sitter for a few hours
  • Names feelings like happy, sad and angry

3. Listening and talking

  • Follows directions with two or three steps
  • Speaks in full sentences
  • Listens to a short story and talks about it
  • Says their first and last name

4. Letters and reading

  • Knows some letters, especially in their name
  • Knows we read from left to right
  • Hears words that rhyme, like cat and hat
  • Holds a book and turns the pages one by one

5. Numbers and shapes

  • Counts out loud to 10 or higher
  • Counts up to 10 objects, touching each one
  • Knows the numbers 1 to 10
  • Names basic shapes and colors

6. Hands and body

  • Holds a crayon with fingers, not a fist
  • Writes or traces their first name
  • Uses child-safe scissors with a grown-up
  • Runs, jumps and hops on one foot

How to use the checklist

  • Check a few skills at a time, during play. Keep it light and fun.
  • Pick 2 or 3 "not yet" skills and practice them for a few minutes a day.
  • Check again in a month. You will be surprised how much your child has grown.

Easy ways to practice at home

Self-care: Let your child pack and open their own snack box at the park. Practice zippers on a big coat first, then on a backpack.

Feelings: Name feelings out loud during the day: "You look proud!" "That made you frustrated." Feelings picture cards make this easier for little ones. Our Feelings and Calm Down Cards were made for exactly this.

Listening: Play "Simon Says" with two-step directions: "Touch your nose, then jump."

Letters: Start with the letters in your child's name. Find them on cereal boxes, signs and books.

Numbers: Count real things: stairs, apples, toy cars. Touch each one as you count.

Hands: Play dough, stickers, tearing paper and coloring all build the hand strength children need for writing.
