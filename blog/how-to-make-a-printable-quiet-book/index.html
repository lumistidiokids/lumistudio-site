How to Make a Printable Quiet Book for Toddlers (No Sewing)

A quiet book is a small book of hands-on activities: matching, sorting, puzzles and simple games your toddler can play by themselves. Classic quiet books are sewn from felt and take many evenings to make. A printable quiet book gives you the same kind of play in about an hour, with paper, a laminator and velcro dots. Here is how to make one at home, step by step.

What is a printable quiet book?

A printable quiet book (also called a busy book or busy binder) is a set of activity pages that you print, laminate and put in a binder. Each page has a task, like "find the shadow" or "build the snowman", and a set of cut-out pieces that stick on with velcro. Children move the pieces, match them and take them off again, so the book can be played over and over.

It is great for quiet time after the nap is gone, for waiting rooms, car rides, church, restaurants and long winter afternoons at home.

What you need

  • Printable quiet book pages (PDF)
  • Cardstock or thick paper, about 65 to 110 lb
  • A laminator and laminating pouches, or self-adhesive laminating sheets
  • Scissors (for the grown-up)
  • Velcro dots, both sides
  • A 1 inch binder or binder rings, and a hole punch
  • A small zip pouch or envelope to store loose pieces

How to make it in 6 steps

  1. Print. Print the pages on cardstock at "actual size" or 100 percent, so every piece fits its place.
  2. Laminate. Laminate the activity pages and the pieces pages. Lamination makes the book last much longer and easy to wipe clean.
  3. Cut. Cut out the pieces along the lines. Leave a small laminated border around each piece, so the edges stay sealed and soft corners do not peel.
  4. Add velcro. Put the hook side on the activity page and the soft loop side on the back of each piece. Use one dot per piece and press firmly.
  5. Bind. Punch holes and put the pages in a binder or on rings.
  6. Store the pieces. Keep a zip pouch in the binder for pieces that are not in use.

Safety first: small pieces and toddlers

Velcro dots and small cut-out pieces can be a choking hazard for young children. Please keep these rules in mind:

  • Make pieces big. Pieces about 2 inches or larger are a good choice for toddlers.
  • Check the book often. Throw away any velcro dot that comes loose.
  • Always stay close while your child plays, especially with children under 3.
  • Keep the pieces pouch out of reach when you are not playing together.

The American Academy of Pediatrics has helpful choking prevention tips for families. If you are not sure if a toy is safe for your child's age, ask your pediatrician.

Quiet book page ideas by age

Ages 2 to 3

  • Color match: put the red mitten on the red square.
  • Shadow match: find the shape that fits each shadow.
  • Big and small: sort pictures into two groups.
  • Build a simple picture: add the hat, eyes and scarf to a snowman.
  • Two or four piece puzzles.

Ages 3 to 4

  • Counting 1 to 5: put the right number of gifts in the sled.
  • Sorting by season: winter clothes or summer clothes?
  • Small, medium and big: put three pictures in order.
  • Mitten pairs: find the two that are the same.
  • Trace a path with a finger or a dry-erase marker.

Ages 4 to 5

  • Letter match: big letters to small letters.
  • Beginning sounds: match the picture to its first letter.
  • Patterns: what comes next?
  • Counting to 10 and simple add one more.

How to use a quiet book so it stays special

Children love a quiet book most when it feels new. Keep it for special times, like quiet time, travel or appointments, instead of leaving it out all day. Show each new page once together first: "Look, the snowman lost his hat. Can you find it?" Then let your child play alone while you stay nearby. Rotate pages every week or two, and add a seasonal set from time to time.

Printable quiet book vs felt quiet book

  • Time: a printable book takes about an hour. A felt book can take weeks.
  • Cost: printable pages, cardstock and velcro cost much less than felt and supplies.
  • Variety: you can print new themes for every season.
  • Feel: felt books are softer and last for years, which is lovely if you sew.

Many families start with a printable book, and keep a felt one as a special gift.

Quick recap

  • Print on cardstock at actual size, laminate, cut and add velcro.
  • Keep pieces big and check velcro dots often.
  • Choose activities that match your child's age.
  • Save the book for special times and rotate the pages.
