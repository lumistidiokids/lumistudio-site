Helping Toddlers With Big Feelings: 10 Calm Down Ideas

Tears over the wrong cup. A meltdown at the store. Big feelings are a normal part of being 2, 3 or 4. Young children feel emotions strongly, but they do not yet have the words or the self-control to handle them alone. They learn it, little by little, with our help.

Why toddlers have big feelings

  • They want independence but can not do everything yet.
  • They do not have enough words to say what they feel.
  • Hunger, tiredness and changes make everything harder.
  • The skills to calm down are still developing.

10 calm down ideas

  1. Stay close and calm. Your calm voice helps their body calm down.
  2. Name the feeling. "You are angry because the tower fell." Naming helps children understand what happens inside.
  3. Breathe together. Smell the flower, blow out the candle. Or blow pretend bubbles.
  4. Squeeze and let go. Squeeze hands into fists, then relax them.
  5. Make a calm corner. A pillow, a soft toy and a calm down poster.
  6. Use feeling cards. Point to a picture: happy, sad, angry, scared. Many kids can point before they can explain.
  7. Move it out. Stomp like an elephant, then walk like a quiet mouse.
  8. Read about feelings. Books and coloring pages about emotions give words for next time.
  9. Talk later. When everyone is calm, talk about what happened and what to try next time.
  10. Notice good moments. "You were so frustrated, and you took a big breath. That was hard!"

When to ask for help

Tantrums are common in toddlers. If they are very frequent, very long, or you are worried, your pediatrician is a good person to talk to.
