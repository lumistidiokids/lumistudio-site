How to Teach a Preschooler to Read: 8 Gentle Steps Before Kindergarten

You do not need flashcard drills or a reading app to help your preschooler become a reader. Most of the skills that come before reading are built through talking, songs, stories and play. A few cozy minutes a day make a real difference.

Here are 8 gentle steps you can start this week.

1. Read aloud every day

Reading together is the best thing you can do for a future reader. Researchers have found that families read with preschoolers less often than before the pandemic, and that homes with more print books read together more often. Keep a basket of picture books where your child can reach it, and read even 10 minutes before bed.

2. Talk about the pictures

Ask "What do you think will happen?" and "How does the bear feel?" This builds vocabulary and understanding, which matter just as much as knowing letters.

3. Play with rhymes

Rhyming helps children hear the small sounds inside words. Sing nursery rhymes, then pause before the last word and let your child fill it in. Try silly rhymes: cat, hat, bat, zat!

4. Start with the letters in their name

A child's own name is the most exciting word in the world. Point to its first letter on signs, cereal boxes and books. Then add the letters of "Mom", "Dad" or a pet's name.

5. Teach letter sounds, not only names

For reading, the sound of a letter is the key. Say "B says /b/ like ball." Keep it short and playful: one or two letters a week is plenty for a 3 year old.

A picture alphabet book helps a lot here. Our My First ABC with Lumi book shows one big, clear picture for each letter, and the ABC Bundle adds matching flashcards and coloring pages.

6. Show how books work

Run your finger under the words as you read, from left to right. Let your child turn the pages. These small things teach that print carries meaning.

7. Make it move

Many little kids learn best when their bodies are busy. Tape letters on the floor and play "Jump to the letter that says /m/". Hide letter cards around the room for a letter hunt.

8. Keep it short and happy

Stop while your child is still having fun. Five to fifteen minutes is enough at this age. If your child pushes back, take a break and come back to stories and songs.

When will my child actually read?

Every child is different. Many children learn to read words in kindergarten or first grade, and that is completely normal. In preschool, the goal is to love books, hear sounds and know some letters.
